Chapter 22
In what world did I ever chase women? Goddamnit. This is why I can't fall in love. The cost of love hurts. It's never as simple as I want it to be.
But I've already been reduced to nothing, what else do I actually have to lose? Snapping back to reality, I felt numb.
"Cheer up son, you look like someone punted your dog." I guess my face betrayed me.
"I don't know pop, I don't feel it."
"Then find it. Now hurry up, let's get on this damn plane. I didn't fake my handicap status for nothing."
The days of private jets and first class flights had ended with "The Chosen". They were hardly ever worth it, but they made me look good.
I followed my dad, shuffling our way onto the plane. I hadn't taken a moment of reflection yet. Everything was still fresh. New. No, not new. Forgotten.
My dad wiggled his way into the seat. Watching him do it was like watching someone try to fit their oversized carry on in the overhead. Fortunately I made him take the window seat, I took the aisle, and left the middle empty. The fight of the night bonuses were going to be better things recently.
"Alright, Son, that was a good fight. A damn good fight." He smiled at me.
"Ya, thanks. I sure felt good."
"So what's next? Champion?"
 "I don't know? Why ask me? Ask Rob."
"Well let's get home and regroup first. You know that kid was a killer right?"
"You told me he was just alright."
"Ya and look at that. I haven't seen you that good since you left my gym." He liked to remind me that things got worse after I left. "
One thing for sure we can't get away with another camp of just me and you."
He was right. I was able to get by on sheer will power last time, but this is the big leagues. Like a woman searching for love, my competition keeps getting younger and I keep getting older. "Save it, pop. I know, I know, when I left I changed."
I'd heard this lecture enough already.
"Oh, did you have any plans tonight?"
His question made me realize I hadn't taken any time for myself since I moved back here. "Uh, no. Why?"
"Oh no reason." "Dad?" I tried to stare at him and let my eyes bore holes through his head. He didn't flinch or even bother to notice me. "What?" The sly bastard was playing coy. The man could be crafty when he needed to be. Or he just knew me too well.
"You'll just have to find out."
The flight attendant came on over the intercom to inform me to prepare for take off.
"This isn't over old man." I spat.
Snore.
Goddamnit how did he fall asleep so fast?
