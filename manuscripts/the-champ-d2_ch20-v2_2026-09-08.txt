Chapter 20
Dad and I sat in the locker room watching the fights.
Hopes and dreams shattered on the tv screen.
Thump.
That was me not too long ago. Broken and withered on the canvas.
He grabbed a pad. We got to work.
“Let’s go, let them fly.”
The sounds of my fists hitting the pads and my powerful exhales echoed off the walls.
“Again.”
Thump. Thump. Thump. Thump.
The sweat ran down my head and face.
It had been years since I fought this far down on a card. The third fight on the main card.
“Good. Good. Shadow box.”
I complied, following my shadow. I weaved in and out of the punches. I looked for my own counters.
“So,” I asked, “Who is this guy anyway?”
“Mills. Young and hungry. He’s tough but not elite. There’s some holes in his game. Overall, he’s solid. He will hurt you if you don’t take him seriously.”
I nodded. I continued to circle around my shadow self.
On the tv, a brutal knockout was replaying.
It was time.
Thump. Thump. Thump.
The staff ushered us down the walkway.
I couldn’t hear the music.
Thump. Thump.
I released myself to the void of the crowd, allowing them to march me to the cage.
The cage looked odd. Like an old friend that had scorned me.
I kicked off my hoodie and shoes. The tape guy did his thing.
My dad grabbed the back of my head and pulled me close, “Go, become you, again.” We embraced.
Mills arrived not too long after. He was different from Rodriguez. Mills was taller, skinnier.
He stared me down from across the cage as the introductions rolled.
“Fighting out of Kingsville, Texas, weighing in at one-hundred-and-fifty-five pounds, Chuck Miles!”
