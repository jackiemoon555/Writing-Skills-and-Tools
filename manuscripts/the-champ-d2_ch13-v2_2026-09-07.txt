Chapter 13
“Look, Chuck, you need to get it together.”
“I mean, no shit, dad.”
“Don’t talk back to me in my home.”
I rolled my eyes, “Yes, I know. Your house, your rules.”
“You’re lucky I even let you in. How on earth did you run through so much money?”
I sighed, “Dad, are you going to chastise me every damn day?”
“Just until you get it together.”
“Alright, man. I’m going out.”
He grunted.
There wasn’t much left in this town. An old place built around a prominent Texas ranch.
I wandered around at first, aimlessly. It had been so long the place didn’t feel the same, but it was the same. Same bars, same people.
I wandered into my old hometown favorite. The people knew me but they respected my privacy, when I chose to sit by myself at the far corner.
I waved my hand. The bartender walked over.
He grunted.
“Double jack on the rocks.”
I didn’t know his name. I never asked and neither did he.
He gave me his affirmative grunt.
He was older, grey haired, he didn’t talk much. He didn’t ask questions, but he always brought me my drink.
I swirled brown liquid in the glass. I followed its flow. I allowed the flow to take me.
Before the abyss could suck me in, a faint voice broke my trance.
“Chuck?” She asked.
I hadn’t looked up yet. I’d wronged too many women.
“Oh my god. It is you Chuck.” She snuck in close and hugged me.
Her scent filled my nose. It was familiar and inviting. Who is she?
I pulled back and looked this woman in her eyes.
“Lisa.” I said.
I was locked in place. She was more beautiful than when I left her.
Fuck.
“I didn’t know you were in town. When you’d get back?” She asked.
“Couple of weeks ago, or something like that.”
“Did you get tired of living the high life in Miami?”
I didn’t answer. I just drank my whiskey.
“Let me guess,” she said impatiently, “Something like that?”
“Bingo.” I said.
She punched me in the arm.
“Well, Chuck, I gotta go, same number?”
“Ya.”
She waved by.
I ordered another drink.
