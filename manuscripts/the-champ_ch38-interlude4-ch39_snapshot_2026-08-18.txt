Chapter 37
I sat on the curb outside of the rehab center.
I had to get out of the small town. I went to San Antonio, a place I chose myself to kill my demons.
It was almost time now.
I pulled another drag from my cigarette, the ash following on me.
I sighed, “God damn it.” I whispered
No more tears. I didn’t deserve to let them fall, for others anymore.
A car arrived in the parking lot, parking a little too close to me. I peeked back, seeing two people in the car. The driver looked recognizable, but I was almost three hours away from home.
I finished the rest of cigarette and mentally prepared for the hardest part. Standing I took my first step towards my own salvation.
Somone caught my arm. A mess of hair pulled me close, holding me tight. I let the moment take me.
I looked down and realized who it was. I kissed the top of my head.
“Why didn’t you come see me?” Lisa asked.
“I couldn’t. I still can’t.”
“You’re such a fool Chuck. I can’t stand it.”
“I know. I don’t have any words for you. I’m doing the only thing I can.”
“That’s good. I’m happy for you.” She wiped her nose.
“Go get well Chuck. We’ll talk when you get out.”
“I love you.”
Don’t Chuck. Not yet. I’m only here because I care about you as a person, and this baby is yours just as much as it is mine.”
“Thank you.”
I jumped off the deep end once again, but for a different purpose this time.
Interlude
Dan Walters sat in his office. Empty and alone.
He kept it barren on purpose. Fighters come and go, can’t get too attached.
It had been so long since he last saw Chuck. His former pupil ravaged fame. He saw it happening again.
His phone rang.
“Who could it be at this time of night?” Dan thought to himself.
He didn’t recognize the number, but he answered anyway. He never knew what opportunity lurked around each corner.
“Hello?”
“Coach!” He knew that voice.
“Chuck?”
“You know it.”
“Oh, wow nice to hear from you. Hell of a knockout kid.”
“Thanks Coach. You’re probably wondering why I called.”
“To be honest Chuck, you’re the first fighter I dropped to call me.”
Chuck chuckled. “That’s not a good sign coach.” I couldn’t help but laugh too.
“So, what do you want Chuck?”
“There he is. No bad blood, I called to ask a favor.”
Dan grunted.
“I want to coach. I want to learn from you.”
Dan didn’t know what to think. Sure, he had assistant coaches, but no one he would leave the reigns to.
“I can’t Chuck. You know how it is. Too many mouths to feed as is.”
“I’m not asking to get paid, I’m asking for your knowledge.”
Dan sighed again. “I’ll get back to you. I can’t promise anything right now.”
“That’s all I’m asking for. Take care coach.” He hung up.
Chuck was one of the first fighters he recruited after his former fights started to age out of the sport.
This rough kid from small town Texas, with an agent who was too big for his britches.
Ding!
He could never get away from the cage.
Ding! Ding!
Chuck had been different. Not the same cocky guy that was starching people left and right.
“He’s no Vlad, champ. Let’s get this done and you back to the strap.” Chuck didn’t react. He wasn’t looking at him.
Ding! Ding! Ding!
The fight was over before it started.
Chuck was out after that. John rose in his stead.
From one prospect to next, he made men into champions.
Now John was taking the same route as Chuck. Just like Chuck, he couldn’t save him from himself.
Ding! Ding! Ding! Ding!
Another flashback appeared in his mind. The day he caught them doing coke in the locker room.
The revelation came almost instantly.
He picked up the phone and let it ring, until the answer came.
“Ok. I’ll take you Chuck.”
Chapter 38
I walked into the bright light. Eyes blinded. The sunlight felt good.
The warmth spread to my heart as I looked at the parking lot.
I smiled crossing the parking lot.
“CHUUUUUUCK.” Carl came running at me, eyes already leaking emotion.
I side stepped him, whispering as I passed, “Love you, too buddy.”
I moved towards my parents. They welcomed me with open arms.
“That’s my boy.” My dad said he was choked up.
“I’m so proud of you.” Mom cried.
“I love y’all.” I blushed.
Looking over their heads, I saw her. Her stomach had swelled to a disproportionate size. I just stared at her, at a loss for words.
My dad spoke, “Go.”
I forced each foot forward. I needed her, even if she rejected me.
Thump.
“H-hi.” I stammered.
“Shhhh.” She hugged me.
“I’m happy you’re back. I’m not ready to give you another chance or if I ever can. But you’re on daddy duty now. Let’s go we have a mommy class to get to.”
I was home, finally.
Thump.
