Chapter 15
The car ride was silent.
Sitting in the back of my childhood mini van, brought me back to simpler times.
“You remember always trying to force me into this damned old thing?”
“Yes, son, I do.”
“Weren’t Carl and I just the worst?”
“Well, just one of you.”
I allowed the silence to resettle.
Each dusty street was the same as it was when the town was built. The downtown area was still built like a classic fifties movie.
The people were much of the same. I remember when I couldn’t fathom being like them. Yet, here I am.
Dad didn’t talk the rest of the trip.
Eventually, I came to realise where we were headed.
He unlocked the door to the gym and motioned me in.
The smell of stale, dank air rushed to meet me. The place always smelled like sweat, even now. The lights flipped on, one by one.
The old mats came to life one more time.
Each light revealing red or blue. I walked to the center of the room and took it all in.
“It’s just like we left it.”
Dad, just grunted.
He was still walking around.
The memories started rushing back to me. Before I could get lost in nostalgia, I heard a whistle.
I turned to see my dad in pads.
“Get up, son. We have work to do.”
“Dad, not right now. I’m not even dressed for training.”
“Shut up. Get on your feet.”
“Dad, stop.” I pleaded.
“I said get on your god damn feet.” His voice deepened.
I sprang to my feet and kicked off my shoes.
“Punch!” He commanded.
I complied.
“Harder!”
I let my hands go.
“Kick!”
I hesitated.
“Harder!”
I let my pain flow into my kick.
“Again!”
I released another combo.
“Harder!”
I felt my disappointment infect my fists.
“Harder!” Dad roared.
I tried again.
“Harder!”
I pushed past my limit. I fell flat on my face, missing the pad entirely.
He threw the pad at me as I landed on the ground.
“Weak, son.” He said, taking the body pad off.
“You’re slow, son. You didn’t learn to fight like that from me.”
I let my breathing answer for me.
Dad threw the pads against the wall.
“I did all this for you and this is how you repay me? You fucking make a fool of yourself. Out every night, doing drugs, drinking, cheating? We raised you better than that.”
He was pacing around the room, “Followed that serpent to Miami and look at what they did to you.”
Dad stood over me, “Get up, son. You’re not done yet. I’ll pick up the pieces, I’ll pick them up every time, because I’m your father. I’m still here, son. Now, I’ll see you at home.”
“What do you mean?” I asked between sobs.
“Get to running.”
