Chapter 16
Dad drove by slowly, “Keep that pace and you’ll never beat my record.” He chuckled before speeding off.
Three days.
The hardest damn three days of my life.
I kept it moving as best I could.
The run back from the gym was six miles in the Texas heat. Not just any Texas heat either, but the humid hellscape of South Texas.
I ran with my shirt off in a pair of shorts. I kept a slower pace as I slowly increased the intensity.
Today, I took a detour. Splitting across a neighborhood, I ran to a local bar.
I tried to walk in, “Chuck?” A surprised feminine voice gasped.
“Lisa! Just who I was looking for.” Behind Lisa an older man was scowling at me. Lisa quickly rushed around the bar, passing an excuse to the man as she walked out.
“Come here, you dumb ass.”
“What?”
“Just cause you’re some kind of big shot doesn’t mean you walk around with no shirt.”
“I figured  you’d like it.”
She tried to hide her laugh, “What are you doing here anyway Chuck?”
“You never called, or text.”
“Oh, well I’ve been busy.” She looked off to the side.
“Lisa.” I said sternly.
She sighed, “I just. I. I. I don’t know Chuck. I saw you the other night and I felt all my old feelings. I just can’t let you in that easy.”
“Damn.” I tried to say under my breath.
“Ok, let’s take a step back. One dinner. If you hate it, then you’ll never see me again.”
She mused, turning her head sideways.
Before she could answer, I started jogging away, “Don’t worry, I’ll call you with the details.”
