## Core theme
A man falls from grace and comes to realize that everyone he loves is better off without him.
The ending is a hybrid: **redemptive for Jack** (his change is real) but **tragic for the
relationship** (it doesn't undo the damage or win anything back). He changes because he's
forced to — losing everything — not because of Van or for her. Success itself is what let his
childhood wound take over and express itself as cruelty; he hasn't been "the real him" since he
found success. Losing everything strips away the conditions (money, status, impunity) that let
the corruption run — which is why the crash can save him instead of just punishing him.

## CORE DEVICE — the erotica IS the barometer (added 2026-08-12)
**The sex scenes are the progress meter for Jack's arc — the book's central instrument.**
- **Start (Ch1–3):** sex is *purely* about Jack — his pleasure, his power, domination and
  degradation (Nicole as "my good slut"). Selfish, transactional, a conquest. This is rock
  bottom of the self, dressed as the top of his life.
- **As he changes:** the erotic content becomes *much more than that* — mutual, intimate,
  connected; sex as genuine relationship rather than domination (the Sam/loving-intimate and
  Pauline/mutual-honest models he encounters chart the shift).
- So the erotica is **not gratuitous — it's the thematic X-ray of his interior.** A reader
  should be able to track Jack's redemption by *how the sex reads*, not just by what he says.
  This is *Forgotten*'s equivalent of *The Champ*'s motif system — the sex is the "motif" that
  measures change. Craft bar: every erotic scene must be doing arc work, or it's decoration.

## Structure
- **Beginning (Ch. 1–4, written):** Sc1 hook (Jack's lifestyle, misses anniversary), Sc2
  inciting incident (Pat's blowup/resignation + Van's call), Sc3 threshold (the crash) — closes
  the Beginning arc.
- **Interludes (multiple tracks, woven through, not front-loaded):**
  - Childhood/family backstory — father's pressure, the groomer. Timeline: backstory starts at
    reference age 16, **no explicit content until Jack's 18th birthday, hard line regardless**
    of how young the implied starting point gets (earlier onset = more control, conveyed via
    brief references, not full scenes).
  - College interlude — origin of Jack+Pat's friendship and Jack+Van's romance, same period.
    Shows the "real" Jack: same charm/drive but grounded, capable of real softness/loyalty — the
    contrast that makes "success corrupted him" land.
- **Middle (Scenes 12, 18, + blank slots):** Journey/picaresque. Jack meets people (romantic and
  not) who each chip away at a piece of his old worldview. His marriage to Van gets filled in
  here too. Blows hit all at once, not spaced for comfort.
- **End (Scenes 25–26, blank):** Sc25 (final confrontation) = changed Jack confronts Van (maybe
  with Pat present) — she still says no. Requires a real Pat–Jack reconciliation first or the
  acceptance reads hollow. Sc26 (resolution) = Jack living *inside* the rejection rather than
  getting past it — the thesis made concrete.

## Characters
- **Jack** — protagonist. Father demanded only greatness; "even when you failed you fell up…
  had this charm." Needs to dominate genuinely *accomplished* people (his father's conditioning).
  Wasn't "himself" once success removed consequences.
- **Patrick (Pat)** — best friend/business partner. Quits over Jack's excess (Ch3). Ends up with
  Van. Met Jack in college. Reconciliation with Jack should be slow/earned given Ch4's rage.
- **Van** — Jack's wife. Leaves after the missed 10-year anniversary. Ends up with Pat. Final
  confrontation: Jack shows up changed, she says no anyway — her own agency, not a scorecard of
  his redemption.
- **Nicole** — intern, Yale-educated ("smartest mind" is load-bearing — evidence of Jack needing
  to dominate accomplished women). Interiority deliberately ambiguous. Possible reappearance
  later as a character "broken" by Jack — or that's someone new (undecided).
- **Sam** — Jack's assistant (seed planted Ch2; fix the "Alice" vs "Samantha/Sam" naming
  inconsistency). Returns in the Middle to reintroduce love/sensuality vs. the Nicole domination
  dynamic. May or may not end up with Jack.
- **Trans woman (journey character, unnamed)** — Jack falls for her; confronts his homophobia.
  Craft flag: avoid the trope of a trans character existing only to teach the lead a lesson then
  removed via harm — give her real, independent wants.
- **Pauline (journey character)** — models a mutual, honest relationship (both using each other,
  no deception), distinct from the Nicole domination. Locked arc: they try to build something
  real; she takes a celibacy oath and stays faithful, but Jack (insecure, attention-starved)
  can't trust it and blows it up himself; he grows jealous of her past and weaponizes it — a
  hypocrisy given his own. Growth is incomplete/non-linear; losing her pushes him back to vices
  (candidate for Sc18 "all is lost"). Open question: what finally breaks the relapse-and-bottle
  cycle, since he's now lost everything more than once.
- **Father** — hard man, demands greatness. Same hedonism as Jack but never blew up publicly
  (mastered compartmentalization). His infidelity drives the stepmother toward Jack.
- **Stepmother (likely the groomer, not finalized)** — motive is the father's infidelity/neglect.
  Timeline hard line holds (see above).
- **Birth mother** — status undecided; doesn't block drafting.

## Relationship to *The Champ*
**Similar premise, different aspects** (author's framing). Both are fall-and-redemption stories
about a talented man undone by a childhood wound that success let express as cruelty. Differences:
*Forgotten* is erotica (sex-as-progress-meter), corporate/business world, a **tragic-for-the-
relationship** ending (Van says no); *The Champ* is MMA, motif-driven (Thump/Knock/Flash), a
**hopeful/reconciliation** ending. Watch: keep them distinct enough that they don't read as the
same book twice (protagonist voice, the specific wound, the ending's emotional colour).
