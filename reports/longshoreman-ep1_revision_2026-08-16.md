PASS: pov / voice   PAGES: Ep1

The episode is third-person close on Vinny and stays there — no head-hopping into Tony. Good.
Three things to know about *how close* you're sitting:

1. **L13** — "Tony's older. Well not older than Vinny's dad, but older than his oldest brother.
   Tony was dressed in a work coat, work jeans, work boots, hard hat, and safety glasses."
   → **Rule: psychic distance.** Close third means the narration sees what Vinny sees, in the
   order and register he'd notice it. A head-to-toe inventory is the camera pulling back to
   "author describing a character." Vinny's known Tony for years — he wouldn't itemize the
   uniform. → *Why it costs:* the reader is yanked out of Vinny's skull for a paragraph, right
   after "Aye, Tony" put them in it. (Cut-or-keep is yours; the rule is: describe what *this*
   POV would actually register — the salt-and-pepper, the same beard.)
2. **L97** — "Vinny thought he could feel the air move around him."
   → **Rule: filter words** (`thought`, `felt`, `saw`, `heard`, `noticed`, `looked`, `watched`,
   `seemed`). They narrate the *perceiving* instead of the *perceived*. "Vinny thought he could
   feel the air move" = two filters between the reader and the air. Compare: *The air moved
   around him.* → *Why it costs:* at the climax, every filter is a pane of glass. This is THE
   line to strip. Also: L93 "until he heard the jingle" (→ the jingle), L95 "He hadn't looked
   around yet" (fine — it's a fact, not a filter), L25 "He slowly looked over the items."
   Checker found 11; the ones at the climax matter most.
3. **L97** — "That's not natural, even in this fucked up world of magic."
   → **Rule: free indirect thought.** Present tense inside past narration is *allowed* when
   it's the character thinking in the moment (that's what this is). It reads as a slip only
   because you also slip tense where it *isn't* thought (L9 "It's what they didn't tell him
   about", L13 "Tony's older", L49 "are ever ominous"). Fix the accidental ones and the
   deliberate one starts reading as deliberate. → CHOICE, protected — once the others are gone.

PATTERNS: filter words at moments of sensation (11 hits) — the pattern to break.
PARKED: tense slips → line pass.

PASS: line / grammar   PAGES: Ep1

**Repeat offender #1 — the vocative comma (8+ times).**
- L15 "Good to see you Vin." · L21 "Fuck you old man." · L27 "Let's see here Vin." · L31 "Don't
  worry Vin" · L33 "I know Tone" · L39 "You know how he started kid?" · L43 "Every fuckin time
  Vin." · L55 "Here it is Vin." · L65 "Contrograts Tone." · L79 "Hey Tone!"
- → **Rule: when you address someone by name (or "kid," "old man"), the name is set off with a
  comma.** "Fuck you, old man." "You know how he started, kid?" (You already do it right at L41
  "Yes, Tone" and L73 "Uh, Tone?")
- → *Why it costs:* without the comma the reader briefly reads the name as the object of the
  verb ("fuck you old man" = fucking an old man; "started kid" = started a kid). It's the
  single most common error in your dialogue and it's a one-keystroke habit.

**Repeat offender #2 — terminal punctuation and quotation marks (3 times).**
- L35 `“He’s always steered us right”` (no period at all) · L45 `“…Let’s get to it already”.`
  (period outside) · L57 `Marlboro Red’s the cowboy killers.`
- → **Rule (US): periods and commas go INSIDE the closing quotation mark, always.** "…get to it
  already." And every line of dialogue ends in some mark.
- → *Why it costs:* it reads as unfinished; it's the first thing a slush reader notices.

**Repeat offender #3 — tense wobble (3 times, non-thought).**
- L9 "It's what they didn't tell him about." · L13 "Tony's older." · L49 "The crashing waves are
  ever ominous."
- → **Rule: pick the narrative tense (you've picked past) and every narrator sentence obeys it.**
  Present is reserved for direct/free-indirect thought (see POV #3).
- → *Why it costs:* each flip is a tiny "who's talking now?" — and it makes your deliberate
  present-tense thought at L97 look like a fourth accident.

**One-offs (with the rule):**
- L7 "The worst part, none of this shit was in the job description." → **Rule: a comma can't
  introduce an explanation; that's a colon or a dash.** "The worst part: none of this…" / "The
  worst part — none of this…"
- L33 "I want to know why we've been moving so much extra weight?" → **Rule: an indirect
  question ("I want to know why…") is a statement — period, not question mark.** "For what?"
  stays a question.
- L53 "Towards the end of the maze of shipping containers they found their target." → **Rule:
  a long introductory phrase gets a comma before the main clause.** "…containers, they found…"
- L57 "Marlboro Red's the cowboy killers." → **Rule: apostrophes never make plurals.** "Marlboro
  Reds, the cowboy killers." (and the appositive wants a comma).
- L59 "He patted on the bottom and pulled out a fresh one. He offered to Toney" → "patted the
  bottom"; and "offered" needs an object (room E1-8) — your word.
- L69 "Left unlocked, and unnumbered." → **Rule: no comma before "and" when it joins just two
  words.** "Left unlocked and unnumbered."
- L75 "Toney mumbled shuffling back to the truck." · L91 "searched Toney's body looking for the
  keys" → **Rule: a trailing participial phrase (-ing) is set off with a comma.** "mumbled,
  shuffling…" / "body, looking…"
- L93 "Frantically he patted down each pocket, until he heard the jingle." → two rules: an
  **introductory adverb takes a comma** ("Frantically, he…") and **no comma before a
  restrictive "until."** (…each pocket until…). Or cut "Frantically" — the patting shows it.
- L49 "Only the never ending dark, only interrupted by the occasional light." → **Rule:
  compound modifiers before a noun are hyphenated.** "never-ending dark." And "only… only" is
  an echo (see below).
- L27 "Me and you, though we got something a little extra tonight." → dialect's fine; the
  comma pair belongs around "though": "Me and you, though, we got…"

**Echoes / filler (checker):** "dark" ×4 in L49 alone (+ "Dark waves… dark environment"); "just"
×5; "started" ×3 (L39, L43 fine — dialogue; L83 "started walking" → walked); "only… only" L49;
"even" ×2. **Rule: filler (`just`, `even`, `almost`, `started to`) is a word that could vanish
without changing meaning.** Read L3–9 aloud with the "just"s out and hear it.
**Adverbs:** L25 "slowly looked over" (the "Line by line" beat already IS slow); L93
"Frantically." L15 "lately," L49 "normally" — fine.

PATTERNS: vocative comma (10) · terminal punctuation inside quotes (3) · tense wobble (3) ·
filter words (11) · participial comma (2). **These five rules cover ~30 of the ~35 marks in
the episode.** Learn these five and Ep1 is 85% clean.
