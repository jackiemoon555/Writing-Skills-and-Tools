**HARDEST FIRST — (1) The prologue has no resistance, so it isn't a scene yet; it's a
requisition form being filled.** The Director opens with "I don't have a choice" and "I've been
given a blank check just for this" — so every ask the priest makes is pre-approved before he
makes it. Logistics: "one and three are easy." Science team: one objection ("Don't the whole
religion and science thing clash"), answered in one line, conceded with "Uh, I guess… not my
money." Nothing is refused, nothing costs, nobody leaves the room with less than they wanted.
The only friction is the Director's contempt, and contempt with a blank check is decoration.
The same thing happens in Ch1: the officer "doesn't know," Johnson doesn't know, the reader
doesn't know — a summons where nobody in the hallway has a stake in the answer. **Lever:**
somebody in each scene has to want something they can't just have. In the prologue that is
either something the priest asks for that a blank check CAN'T buy (the cross is that move — and
it's thrown away in two lines, "Fat chance" / "Worth a shot"), or something the Director
refuses to give up. In Ch1, give one of the two men a guess about what the suit wants — a wrong
one is fine — so the walk has a direction. Do not write toward explanation; write toward a
refusal.

**(2) NOT WORKING / SHOW-vs-TELL — the premise is asserted, never glimpsed.** "any of this
bullshit" · "this bullshit" · "this whole fucking sharade" · "reality is often weirder than
fiction" · "adjust to their reality." The thing that forced a President to hand a priest the
US military is referred to five times and shown zero. Withholding is a legitimate choice; the
problem is that the vagueness reads as the WRITER not knowing yet rather than the CHARACTER
refusing to say. **Lever:** one concrete object in the room that proves the thing happened — a
photo in the manila folder, a number, a place name the Director won't say twice — without
explaining it. The reader needs one real noun to believe the abstraction.

**(3) PACING — the button is delayed by its own explanation.** "It was a question he had put
off all weekend. Since the inception of this whole fucking sharade, he reluctantly answered,
'Task Force Cryptid.'" The name is the prologue's punchline and it lands — but two sentences
of telling ("put off all weekend," "reluctantly") sit between the President's question and the
answer, and they say what the pause would say by itself. The President exchange before it
("I trust the meeting went well." / "We've had the groundwork laid as we speak.") is filler
whose only job is to reach the question. **Lever:** words go where the tension is — let the
question hit and the answer come, and let the reluctance live in the beat, not the adverb.

**(4) CH1 — SHOW-vs-TELL at the turn, and the strongest page ends on air.** "Before he could
act, he was saved." pre-announces the officer's entrance one line before the entrance shows it
("Bursting through the door came an officer"). The saving is the show; the sentence before it
is the caption. Same at the close: "Johnson sighed. What the fuck was it now?" — the "now"
promises a history (the late night, "something like that") that the page withheld, so the
chapter ends on a question the reader hasn't been given a reason to care about. **Lever:**
cut the caption, let the door do it; and either let one detail of the late night onto the page
or cut "now" — a hook needs something under it.

**(5) DIALOGUE / REPETITION — the Director is one note; the priest and the officer aren't.**
Director: "bullshit" ×2, "Ya, ya. Whatever," "Crock of shit," "not my money," "Fat chance" —
generic-cynic lines any cynic could say. The priest's lines are specific to HIM ("I can't
phone God for his detailed analysis of his structures"; the prayer-handshake). The officer's
are specific to HIM (the daughter warning). The Director is the only one of the three who is a
type instead of a person, and he's the POV of the prologue. **Lever:** one line that only THIS
director would say — about the President, the weekend, the money, anything — instead of the
third "bullshit." OBSERVED, one line: "Ya, ya. Whatever" / "Ya, something like that" is the
Champ's "Ya sure, whatever" cadence walking into the new book on page one. Not a rule here
(4b). Just noticed.

**SPINE / THEME PRESSURE-TEST:** none declared, none testable — discovery draft. What the
page is actually building, unasked: institutional contempt vs. belief (the man with no choice
funding the man of faith), and a boot with a past being pulled into "the community." The
genre engine (government cryptid unit) is a known shape (X-Files, Hellboy/BPRD, SCP,
Ghostbusters); the pages don't yet have the thing that's HIS in it beyond the priest's dry

**BIGGEST-SCENE / FEWEST-WORDS AUDIT:** Prologue — the requisition talk (logistics / science /
special forces) = 194 words; the handshake-prayer, the one beat where two beliefs actually
touch = 54; the President call + the naming, the prologue's only reveal = 74. Ch1 — the hazing
setup = 168; the hallway walk = 197; the actual content of the summons ("What's this about" →
"besides what they might do to you") = 58. The pattern is present on page one: the turns get
