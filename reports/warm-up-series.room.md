**WHAT'S ON THE PAGE:** Joe Lang, therapist ("his smile wide and professional"), meets Mr. Gomez
— gold crucifix, salt-and-pepper hair, Armani, "Buenos Noches"; stress "in my line of business";
"Drugs, alcohol, women, gambling"; a rival's "hostile take over"; "My girl, she left"; brother
killed → tissues → **"what's done is done. The problem was promptly sorted."** → "Just hired some
people to see who did it" → **"It was the first time I cried in my life."**; the pen ("The sound
of pen to paper filled the room"); the watch: no face, "cost me more than a normal house… It's
plain yet it's worth a human life." → **"Everyone has a price Mr. Lang, even you… You've become
somewhat of a big name in my line of work."** → "brush shoulders with some of your clients" →
Joe's face tightens; "Rapid pen sounds crashing"; Joe tries to wrap up; **"A very quiet tap began
from under Joe's desk"**; the manila envelope — "the paint doctor confidentiality thing"; "I'll be
sure to recommend you to my colleagues"; alone: string, dump, a stack of hundreds, files — "Joe
Lang. From Denver Colorado." — "4445 Main street. Married once, one kid, a daughter. Single
parent." — a polaroid: **"He recognized the smile in the photo, it shined just like his."** END.

**VERDICT:** a complete scene with a real ending, and the ending is the best part. The one thing
holding it back is that the piece doesn't know what Joe already knows.

**HARDEST FIRST — (1) The title says Joe IS the black market therapist; the page plays him as a
man finding out.** Gomez came by referral from Joe's clients; "a big name in my line of work" —
Joe has been treating these men for a while. But the dread is spent on Joe discovering what
Gomez is: "his eyes portraying a darkness that lived within," the water stuck in his throat at
the watch, "Joe's face tightened" at "brush shoulders with some of your clients." If he already
knows what his clients are, none of that should move him; what should is NEW — the address, the
daughter, the photo. The fear is spread across the session and the envelope gets what's left.
**Lever:** decide what Joe knew when Gomez walked in; spend the dread only on what's news to him.

**(2) The watch speech is Gomez stating the theme.** "It's plain yet it's worth a human life" →
"Everyone has a price Mr. Lang, even you." He says it to PRICE Joe, so it half-earns its place
and the envelope proves it — but the second line is the thesis in the villain's mouth; the watch
alone was doing it.

**(3) CORNY:** "his eyes portraying a darkness that lived within."

**(4) REPETITION — the smile is planted twice before it pays:** "his smile wide and
professional" → "his smile still warming the room" → the last line. The payoff is the best line
in the piece; it needs the smile once at the top.

**(5) DIALOGUE:** Gomez's voice IS the piece — "how can I put this, is attempting a hostile take
over," "The problem was promptly sorted," "there would be no more issues." Joe's therapist-speak
("channel your energy into something a little more positive") is a lecture — right for a man
hiding behind his job. "the paint doctor confidentiality thing": if it's Gomez's English it's a
great line; if it's a typo for "patient," keep it anyway.

**BIGGEST-SCENE / FEWEST-WORDS AUDIT:** session ≈ 700 · the envelope sequence ≈ 150. The climax
is the shortest section and it is NOT sprinted — seven steps (string, dump, cash, file, address,
"one kid, a daughter," polaroid), no caption. Under 1,000 words, he reached the ending on a full
tank. Noted for the record: a warm-up this length is climax practice for the finishing problem.

**WORKS — PROTECT:**
- The pen as percussion: "The sound of pen to paper filled the room." → "Rapid pen sounds
  crashing throughout the room." → "A very quiet tap began from under Joe's desk." Joe's state

- "It was the first time I cried in my life."
