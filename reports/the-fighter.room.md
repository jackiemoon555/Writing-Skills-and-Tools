## 1. Calibration (register baseline, per voice)

- **Voice A — Chuck (main).** First person. Intended **present tense**; in practice
  drifts present↔past between chapters (see F-TENSE). Punchy, fragment-heavy,
  profane, boisterous MMA voice. Personifies emotions (depression=lion,
  anxiety=predator). Percussive motif **"Thump."** = heartbeat.
- **Voice B — John / "Johnny Law" (Interlude 1).** Third person, past tense.
  Deliberately distinct from Chuck's first person — the POV shift *is* the device.
  Percussive motif **"Flash."** = cameras/fame.
- **Structural motif system (CHOICE, protected):** Thump (heart/fear) · Knock
  (collapse — parents' door, cop's knuckles, Ch12–13) · Flash (fame — Interlude).
  One percussive motif per emotional register. Keep.

## 3. Findings (consolidated; mechanical items already handed back in-session)

- **[F-TENSE] STRUCTURAL — present/past drift** across Chuck's chapters (present in
  Ch1/2, past in Ch3/5/6/7; Ch8 walkout past vs Ch1 walkout present). Disproof
  (present=cage, past=reflection) FAILS: Ch2 reflection is present, Ch8 walkout is
  past. TRIAGE: DEFECT (or convert to a deliberate system). READER-MEDIUM /
  AUDIT-HIGH. Flag for author. The Interlude's clean 3rd-past could *anchor* a
  deliberate system: 1st-present = Chuck-now, 3rd-past = interludes.
- **[F-POV] DEFECT — Interlude 1 POV slip.** "He moved **my** gaze to the terrace"
  → "his." First person leaked into the third-person interlude; breaks the device.
  AUDIT-HIGH. Highest-priority line fix.
- **Mechanical/typos** — full lists delivered in-session (early chapters M1–M14;
  new Ch9–13 batch; Interlude batch incl. `laid`→`lay`, `bestfriend`,
  `infomericial`, `Whats`). Parked by author until first draft is done.
- **[F-TELL] TASTE (protected)** — emotions frequently named/personified rather than
  dramatized. Consistent = voice. Left alone; noted as the spot where showing the
  body would hit harder.

## 4. Test Audience — verdict over the whole arc so far

Seated from the book's promises (first-person MMA fall-and-addiction tragedy),
plus one neglected. Simulated perspectives, not real readers.

- **Men & boys (action):** Winning them. Fights, KOs, the fast fall pull hard.
  They drift where interiority stalls the drive (the Ch3 shower-depression stretch).
- **Soldiers & veterans (the cost):** Earned. The panic attack, "sanctuary burned
  down," the body remembering (grabbing his ribs) — the price of violence reads real.
- **Fathers / the counted-on:** Split. Being *let down* and dad's shame land hard;
  Chuck as a man others *rely on* is underdrawn — he mostly takes.
- **The exhausted at day's end (page-turner):** Mostly worth staying awake. Short
  chapters + motifs turn pages; the Ch12–13 intercut either grips or confuses.
- **NEGLECTED — women / teenage-girl readers (love, being seen):** The book's
  biggest untapped audience. Nicole is scenery ("girls come and go," "wanted a
  piece," influencer caricature). Interlude 1 cracks a door (John's longing) but
  Nicole herself has zero interiority. A whole readership left at the door. Not a
  defect at 20% — a flag for who this book could also be for.
