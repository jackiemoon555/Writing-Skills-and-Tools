# Self-Publishing Playbook — for *The Champ* and every book after

*Compiled 2026-08-12. This is a REFERENCE doc for the publishing phase — revisit after
the draft is done, revisions are done, and an editor is found. Nothing here is urgent
during drafting. Standing rule respected throughout: research/strategy only — the author
writes all his own copy (blurb, ads, taglines).*

**Locked decisions (from this session):**
- **Path: self-publish via Amazon KDP, backlist-first.** Traditional / agents / small-press
  explored and set aside (too slow, novella-unfriendly, off-strategy). Let opportunity come
  to him off a proven catalog later, from a position of strength.
- **Cadence: ≥ 1 book/month** (drafting cadence). Ship no matter what. Profit is not the
  near-term goal — the backlist is.
- **Pipeline:** draft → bounded revision (a fixed # of passes + a stop rule) → editor →
  publish (KDP) → repeat.

---

## PART 1 — EDITORS

**Four types, always in this order:**
1. Developmental (structure/character) — *covered by his own tools + dual reviews.*
2. Line (sentence flow/voice) — *largely covered.*
3. **Copyedit** (grammar/consistency) — **pay for this.**
4. **Proofread** (final typo sweep) — **and this.**

**Cost for a ~35–38k novella** (rates are per-word, so ~half the 80k-novel figures you'll see):
- Copyedit (~$0.01–0.03/word): **~$350–$1,050**
- Proofread: **~$200–$500**
- **Budget path (copyedit + proofread): ~$500–$1,500** total.
- Full developmental edit would add ~$1,000–$2,800 — **skip** (already covered).

**Where to hire (best → cheapest):** Reedsy (top ~3% vetted, filter by genre — he already
uses it for writing) · EFA / the-efa.org (directory + job board, often cheaper) · Upwork/Fiverr
(cheapest, vet hard — ask for a sample edit of the first 1,000 words) · r/selfpublishing.

**Minimum bar (this is a marketing item too):** at least ONE cheap human proofread per book.
"Reviews mention typos" is a reputation wound that follows the author name across the whole
catalog — the cheapest ad he'll ever buy.

## PART 2 — MARKETING & LAUNCH (KDP, no-audience debut)

**Headline:** for a debut with no audience, chasing readers one at a time is negative-ROI.
The best marketing is the next book. BUT "passive is fine; unfurnished is not" — spend a
small, one-time cost on assets that touch *every future reader*.

**DO at launch (in order of leverage):**
1. **A cover that signals genre at thumbnail size.** $100–300 premade/budget-custom from a
   designer who works in dark/emotional drama. Do NOT DIY. The single highest-leverage dollar.
2. **Blurb built on the HOOK** (the fall / DUI / father / the climb back), not the themes.
   *(His words — structure guidance only.)*
3. **KDP setup:** 7 keywords + 2 *niche* categories (researched against what sports-drama/
   men's-fiction readers actually type), price **$2.99** (the 70%-royalty threshold), and
   **label the length honestly as a novella** (mispackaged length = #1 source of punitive
   reviews on short books).
4. **KDP Select / Kindle Unlimited for book 1** — exclusivity costs nothing with no wide
   audience; page-reads suit a novella; free/Countdown promo days get useful once book 2 exists.

**THE ONE MUST-DO:** install the **back-matter → mailing-list pipeline** in book 1 BEFORE it
ships. A final page sending readers to a free signup (MailerLite tier, $0) + an "also by /
coming next" page. The only audience he *owns* — Amazon rents readers; a list keeps them. Even
15 readers/book compounds into the crowd that shows up for book 5.

**IGNORE at rep #1:** paid ads (the math can't work on one book — ~$3–5 spent to earn ~$2, no
catalog to recoup), marketing agencies (scam-dense — see below), paid review services, press
releases, blog tours, Goodreads giveaways, preorders, and social-platform-building (a second
job that eats the 750 words/day, which is the real engine).

**Paid levers — re-time, don't delete.** Amazon Ads ($5–10/day, per-click, no royalty cut) and
promo-site features (Freebooksy-tier flat fee first; BookBub Featured Deals are a lottery for
debuts) become rational at **3+ books in one lane**. Put them on the calendar for that TRIGGER
CONDITION, not a date.

**Marketing companies — the answer to "cut or lump sum?"**
- Self-serve ad platforms + promo sites: **no royalty cut**, flat/per-click fees you control.
  KDP always pays the author directly.
- Freelance publicists / agencies: **big lump sums** (~$500–2,500 freelancer; $3,500–$15,000+
  agency). Not worth it for a debut novella.
- 🚩 Anyone asking for a **% of royalties or your rights** = major red flag. Legit book
  marketing is fee-for-service, never a cut.
- **Scam red flags** (Authors Guild): they cold-emailed YOU; big upfront fee for vague
  deliverables ("media exposure," an Oprah/TV "spot"); one-year contract then they ghost.
  Much of it is the ghostwriting-mill ecosystem. Avoid.

**Fable (the reading APP, not the AI model):** social book-club platform (~3M users). Indie
titles enter its store via **Draft2Digital**, which means going **WIDE** — and wide conflicts
with **KU exclusivity**. So "get on Fable" is coupled to the wide-vs-KU decision, not a free
add-on. Free community engagement (joining clubs) is available now with no distribution; store
placement is premature (also: recently acquired by Everand/Scribd — direction uncertain).

## PART 3 — POSITIONING (*The Champ* specifically)

- **NOT literary fiction packaging** — worst-converting Amazon category, trad-loyal readers.
  The literary bones (two selves, chorus, motifs) are a **review-quote asset later**, not a shelf.
- **NOT romance** — profitable "MMA fiction" on Amazon is mostly MMA *romance* for women (strict
  HEA/couple-POV contracts); *The Champ* breaks all of them and would recruit 1-star reviewers.
- **The honest shelf: sports drama / comeback fiction / men's fiction.** Audience proven mostly
  in FILM — *Warrior, Rocky, Southpaw, The Wrestler.* In books it's thin (F.X. Toole's *Million
  Dollar Baby*, Leonard Gardner's *Fat City*, Thom Jones). Thin demand = a warning; near-zero
  keyword competition = the opening (underserved niches are where a no-name debut can rank).
- **Frame = the fall and the comeback**, not the sport, not the craft. "*Warrior* for readers"
  as the positioning *concept* (author writes the words).
- **Honest expectation:** niche book. Success = "find its 200 right readers," not "break out."

## PART 4 — STRATEGIC TENSION TO DECIDE (the important one)

**Genre-agnostic vs. the backlist engine.** "Backlist = marketing" works through *read-through*
(a happy reader buying the next *similar* book). A genre-agnostic catalog has near-zero
read-through — 12 unrelated books = 12 separate debuts. His two pillars ("genre-agnostic" +
"backlist-as-engine") are in tension.

Not a mandate to change — genre-agnostic serves the reps/craft goal (the real goal). But choose
it knowing the cost. Cheap mitigations if he wants read-through:
- **Cluster:** 2–3 books per lane before switching genres.
- **Pen-name lanes** later (one name per genre).
- **Connected books:** *The Champ*'s own interlude cast (Nicole, Rob, John…) is a ready-made
  connected-book engine for cheap read-through — his call.

**Also:** define launch success as PROCESS metrics (shipped on time, package quality, list live,
lessons logged), written down BEFORE launch — niche-debut sales will be single-to-low-double
digits and that number means nothing if decided in advance. Morale protection = cadence protection.

**Infrastructure:** consistent author name + bio + Amazon Author Central page, set up once. The
catalog is the brand; the label on it must be stable from book 1.

## PART 5 — THE TRADITIONAL OPTION (parked reference)

Set aside for now; kept here in case a *novel* + real sales later make it worth revisiting.
- **Small-press / no-agent (the only realistic trad lane for a novella):** presses like
  Unsolicited Press, Black Lawrence Press (novella contests; open reading June/Nov), Bellevue
  Literary Press. Directories: Reedsy novella publishers, Kindlepreneur, Writing Workshops,
  Poets & Writers, NewPages. Seasonal reading periods; slow (months). Pitch as "literary fiction
  with an MMA backdrop" for these.
- **Agent → Big 5 (a NOVEL path, not novella):** QueryTracker (agent DB + QueryManager portal),
  Manuscript Wish List (MSWL), Reedsy agent directory. Query = ~300–400 words (hook + pitch +
  2 comps + short bio), personalized, sent in small batches. Agents rarely take standalone
  novellas — revisit with a full novel + KDP sales behind it.
- **Hard rule:** legit agents/publishers never charge the author. Money flows TO the author.
  Upfront "reading fees" / "packages" = vanity press. Avoid.
