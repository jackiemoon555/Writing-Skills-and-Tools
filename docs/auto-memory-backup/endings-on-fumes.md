Alec's realization while finishing *The Champ*'s first draft (2026-08-20): why finishing is hard for him isn't discipline — it's **sequencing**. He runs out of juice right at the end, so the climax and last image (the most load-bearing scenes) get written on fumes. This is the mechanism behind his standing pattern "biggest scenes get the fewest words."

**Why:** the ending is where the tank is emptiest, and it's also where the writing matters most — the two collide. On *The Champ* the drafted "John falls apart, Chuck holds him" collapse landed offscreen (one sob, then banter) because he pushed to finish tired.

**How to apply:** protect endings — the climax and final image deserve a FRESH session, never the tail of one. Finish setup on fumes if needed, never the payoff. When intaking a rushed ending, the read is usually "needs more / biggest scene fewest words," not a line issue.

Also his standing practice (2026-08-20): **freeze the first-draft snapshot as-is for later before/after comparison** — revisions become new objects beside it, never overwrite the original in `manuscripts/`. Related: [[working_rules_summary]].
