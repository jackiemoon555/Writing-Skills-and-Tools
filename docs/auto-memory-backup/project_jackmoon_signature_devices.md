Alec (Jack Moon) is consciously building FORMAL SYSTEMS as his authorial signature. On record in
The Champ (as of 2026-08-23): (1) per-character percussion motifs (Thump/Knock/Flash/Thud/Ding +
crowd chants), one per nervous system, direction-coded (descending count = settling, ascending =
panic); (2) CONVERGENT TENSE — past for the protagonist's fall, present for interludes, narration
joins the present at one unannounced seam (~rehab) so the grammar enacts "I'm still here." He
called this "maybe a staple of mine" — treat these as his developing style across future projects,
not one-book tricks. Rulings live in reports/the-fighter.room.md.

ADDENDUM (2026-08-24, recognized by author mid-conversation): his CORE THEME across all
projects — **cycles, and whether a man can break one for the next in line** (who stays vs.
leaves; what gets passed down; "I'm still here"). Data points: The Champ (cast = possible
futures of one man; towel/son = the break), the Future essay ("I Hate That I Love It" —
loving what destroys), Longshoreman (fathers' burdens, inheritance), the janitor story
(ex-cultist mid-cycle-break), the John Wall fuel touchstone, his read of Archer's ending
and Lanterns' Hal/John foil. Use as a compass on story decisions: the choice that serves
the cycle question is usually his answer.
