# Character Designs & Notes

*Captured from a working session. Everything here is built on one core principle
I kept returning to, so it's worth stating up front:*

> **The gift and the wound are the same thing. There's no version of the power
> that doesn't cost you yourself.** Bound every power to a real, permanent cost.
> Harden the load-bearing rules (how it fails, what it costs); leave the stats
> (durations, exact numbers) soft so the plot can guide them.

---

## The Design Rule I Keep Using

For any power, the fix for "this is unbounded / a cheat" is the same three moves:

1. **Narrow it to a specific mechanism** — not "he's fast," but *how* and *within what limits*.
2. **Fence its domain** — define what it explicitly *cannot* do, especially human/uncomputable things.
3. **Make it cost something real and permanent** — ideally aimed *inward* (at the character), so the power is the source of their undoing, not the solution to the plot.

Control is the enemy. Most of these characters only work because they **can't
freely choose** when/how the power comes. The tragedy and the coherence both
live in the loss of control.

## 1. The Catabolic Speedster

A speedster that actually works, because the ceiling is capped where coherence survives.

- **Speed:** capped at roughly car/highway speed (~80mph sustainable). Fast enough to be genuinely dangerous and unoutrunnable on the ground; slow enough that physics doesn't instantly kill him.
- **Fuel = catabolism:** his body burns to move. **Burns only when he runs** — he's a normal man otherwise and has to *prepare/pre-load* to use it. Not always-on; can be caught unfueled and is then just a guy.
  - Body is locked toward breaking down, not building. Burns fat first, then muscle — heavy use makes him waste away and eventually eats the very muscle that produces the speed (self-limiting in the cruelest way).
  - Manages it with a huge high-calorie, high-fat diet, but the **margin is thin** — miss meals, get cut off from food, or overuse, and the cost comes due. The world can break his management at any time.
- **The redline (sacrifice mechanic):** he *can* push past his sustainable top speed (80 → 100+), but doing so **actively kills him** — burns tissue he doesn't get back. This is his "Eighth Gate": an established, known, lethal cost he can choose to pay for something worth it.
- **Geometry weakness:** can't super-speed in tight spaces. Hitting a wall at 60 = dead or severely hurt. He needs **open room** to build and shed speed. Trap him in a building full of walls and he's a fish in a barrel. His weakness is *terrain*, and being cornered is his nightmare.
- **Hard counter:** someone who can *calculate* his movement — an elite marksman/mind who can solve a high-velocity intercept and put a bullet where he'll be. (See the Math Savant — they're natural foils, unengineered.)

**Open knob (leave to plot):** burst vs. cruise. Leaning toward short violent bursts — a few devastating seconds, then spent, gasping, vulnerable, refueling.

## 2. The Math Savant / Reality-Perceiver

Solves the super-intelligence problem by making intelligence a **specific, bounded,
legible skill** instead of an unfalsifiable halo.

- **Power:** he perceives the *mathematical structure of reality* — can almost literally see the load paths and math behind how a building is built, and by extension the physics/probability under everything (trajectories, force, intercepts).
- **He can't turn it off.** It's an affliction, not a tool. He *suffers* the gift.
- **Domain fence (critical):** math is not wisdom. He can compute the *computable* — ballistics, geometry, probability, mechanics — but **cannot** read a lie, predict a human choice, or see a genuinely novel situation. Human/uncomputable things are invisible to him. This is what stops it being a plot-solving valve.
- **The drift (inward cost):** the deeper the math leads him, the further he drifts from reality itself — toward the level where reality stops being solid and becomes probability/uncertainty (the real frontier: Gödel's incompleteness, Heisenberg uncertainty — *use as flavor, do NOT over-explain*). No mind can live there. Following it costs him his footing.
- **The double loneliness:**
  - *Internal* — he's leaving reality, drifting toward the incomprehensible.
  - *Relational* — **people can't see what he sees.** He's fluent in a language no one else speaks, exiled in plain sight. He's right here and no one can follow him six inches down. This also unnerves people and pushes them away, deepening it.
- **Marksmanship (downstream of the power, not a separate skill):** he's Deadshot-level accurate because he *sees the firing solution* — the bullet's parabola, windage, the intercept on a moving target. He doesn't aim; he reads the answer off reality. This is a *better* justification than Deadshot's "he just never misses." Still bounded: needs the hand, the weapon, line of sight, space; can't shoot his way out of being tackled; can't predict a target who ducks for human reasons.

**Craft note (this is a gift for me):** I'm bad at math, which is *ideal* — I
literally can't over-explain it, so I'm forced to write the *effect* and the
*feeling*, never the equations. Render him from the outside and from the
alienation. He wouldn't explain it either — that's his tragedy. Grab ~3 real
terms (uncertainty, unprovable truths, probability underneath everything) for
flavor, used correctly, and stop there.

**Open question:** still reaching for connection and hurt every time the gulf
reasserts, or already retreated fully into the math? (Reaching = heartbreaking;
retreated = colder, further gone.)

## 3. The Fractured Man (Split-Personality Powers)

The DID-as-power idea, saved from the lazy "toolkit" version.

- **Setup:** fractured into distinct selves, **one power each.** Each self is a *person* — own fears, relationships, sense of self — not "the strong one" / "the fast one."
- **No control over the switch (the fix):** selves surface on their own — triggered by stress/trauma, at the worst times, against his will. The power *happens to him*. The tension is "which self is about to seize control, and is it the one I need or the one that'll get me killed?" (If he could choose the switch, it's a cheat. He can't.)
- **The Ultimate Move (the gem):** he can piece all the selves together and use every ability at once by becoming **complete/whole** — but wholeness and power are *opposites* for him. His power comes *from* the fracture, so:
  - Integrating = using everything at once.
  - But being whole **erases the powers**, because the fracture that created them is healed.
  - It's also a kind of murder/mercy — merging dissolves distinct identities that each think they're real and want to live.
- **The choice = the thesis:** *Would you stay broken to keep your power, or become whole and lose what made you special?* His greatest strength requires his greatest wound.

**Open knob (leave to plot / his role):**
- **Version A** — the merge erases powers **permanently**. One-time nuclear option. He comes out whole and ordinary, powers gone forever. Best as a **climax/ending** — the final sacrifice is giving up power *by becoming sane/whole*. (Leaning this way — permanent, load-bearing, Watchmen-tier.)
- **Version B** — merge erases powers **temporarily**; he has to **re-fracture** (lose himself again, on purpose) to get them back. Repeatable, darker as ongoing *torture*, less final.

Solid concept with enough to be guided by the plot. Don't over-specify before it has a home.

## 4. The Bipolar / Damage-Absorber (the heavy one)

The strongest and most personal of these. Power scales with mood; the meds are
the kryptonite; the ending is honest.

### Base power
- **Absorbs damage and re-releases it.** Legible, concrete.
- **Magnitude scales with mood.** Manic high = absorbs enormous punishment, returns devastating force (a walking bomb, but with the worst judgment). Depressive low = fragile, can barely take or give a hit.
- The power has *weather*, and the weather isn't his to command. Manic power coincides with manic recklessness — **most dangerous to enemies and to himself at the same moment.**

### The meds = kryptonite (the dilemma)
- **On his meds (as he should be) → stable, healthy, ordinary → no powers.**
- **Off his meds → mania returns → power returns → but so does the recklessness, the destruction, the danger to himself and everyone he loves.**
- He *can* choose power (stop the meds), but choosing it means choosing to be ill, and being ill means the mania is driving. The only lever he can pull is the one that unleashes what he can't control. **Every heroic act is a relapse.**
- The meds are the *right* choice. The power is the seductive *wrong* one. The pull to go off is the pull to feel like himself, to feel *alive*, to *matter* — even knowing it destroys him. (This is the true, non-glib version. Never frame going off the meds as aspirational. The healthiest thing he could do is never use the power at all.)

### The exploitation layer (Charles Haley / institutional)
- His power only exists when he's unmedicated → **anyone who wants his power has an incentive to keep him sick** (and uninformed about his own condition).
- Modeled on Haley: undiagnosed for years, reportedly medicated by teams that didn't tell him what the pills were for; tolerated and used *as long as he produced*, then discarded with the damage when he stopped.
- Reframes the tragedy: not just self-destruction, but a man destroyed by a system that valued his power over his life, kept him sick on purpose, never let him know he had a choice. **Same spine as everything I write: the individual ground up by the structure that found their damage useful.**

### The CTE layer (compounding horror)
- His power *is taking hits.* Using it = absorbing physical/head trauma = **causing CTE.** The power self-inflicts the decline.
- **CTE accelerates and wildens the mood cycle** → swings get faster, bigger, less controllable → power becomes more extreme *and* less controllable → deteriorates faster while **feeling stronger** (bigger swings read as both "ascending" and "dying" — they're the *same signal*). The thing that looks like getting more powerful is the disease.
- **Closed spiral:** use power → CTE → faster/wilder cycle → bigger, less controllable power → more use → faster spiral. No stable state. The only way out is to stop (meds, stable, powerless), and he almost certainly can't.

### The ending
- **He pays the ultimate price. Self-destructs off the meds.** An immense high that ends in the darkest low — the true bipolar arc. The math is closed; it was always going to end here.
- **Aim the tragedy at the WASTE and the LOSS, not the act.** The devastation lives in what's lost, who he hurt, the ordinary life that was always available and he could never choose. The meds were *right there.* Wholeness was *always* an option. The heartbreak is that he wasn't doomed — he *chose* the thing that doomed him, because being medicated felt like being no one.
- **Clarity at the bottom:** the darkest low comes with brutal clarity — "I could have just stayed well… and they never even let me know I had the choice." That clarity keeps the story from ever saying the destruction was *good*. It says: this was the waste. It didn't have to happen.

**Personal note to self:** writing his crash is not a forecast for me. He goes all
the way down so I don't have to. Loop the care team in when writing the dark
passages; write them on steady days. Keep bipolar disorder and CTE *distinct in
my own understanding* even though they *blur in his experience* — the blur is
HIS horror, not a confusion in my craft. Author clarity, character confusion.

## Institutional / Thematic Material (the CTE thread)

Not a character — the *world/antagonist logic* several of these share. Real, and
it's the same spine as *No More Heroes* and Marshal Law.

- **The unbeatable machine.** The right antagonist for this isn't a villain — it's a *diffuse institutional machine* (money, cultural sanctity, lawyers, bought experts) that can outlast, outspend, and discredit anyone. Dissidents chip at a monolith; nobody cleanly "wins." Bleaker and truer than triumph. **No tower to climb, no nemesis to kill — just incentives doing what incentives do.**
- **The safeguard becomes the weapon.** The thing built to protect people gets turned into the instrument of their exploitation — and it barely takes effort to flip it. (The registry becomes the kill-list. The Hippocratic oath becomes the alibi.) *This is my recurring structure. Use it.*
- **The compromised insider.** The advocate/expert who exposed the machine, then went to work for it. Strongest when you **don't resolve** whether he's a coward, a pragmatist, or a genuine reformer — when even he can't tell if he betrayed the cause or is the only one changing it from inside. More damning than a clean sellout, because it implicates the system's ability to absorb even good-faith opposition. (Keep separate: profiting from *telling* the truth ≠ profiting from *concealing* harm. Don't launder the real villains by flattening everyone into "they all cashed in.")
- **Corruption scales with scarcity.** The core insight. Buying a *common* professional is a small harm; buying a *rare* one is catastrophic, because there was never much of that expertise to begin with and it's non-replaceable. Corrupt half of a rare, vital specialty and you've pointed a meaningful fraction of humanity's *total capacity to understand something* at concealment instead of discovery. The opportunity cost is civilizational — every hour an elite mind spends manufacturing doubt is an hour not spent on the knowledge that would help everyone. **The rarer the skill, the greater the breach of trust when it's misused, because more was riding on so few.** Niche expertise is a *vulnerability*, not a protection, against lopsided money — you don't have to capture everyone, just the handful qualified to speak the truth.
- **No accountability.** The deniers were never really punished — kept on for years, no lost licenses, no ruin. The machine protects its own. That absence of justice is more disturbing than any villain getting away once; it's a *system structurally incapable of punishing the harm it's built on.*

**Reference (for tone / accuracy, not gospel):** *League of Denial* (the objective
account I liked). Real cast to keep straight: denialist insiders (Elliot Pellman,
Ira "Dr. No" Casson); early advocate who deserves credit (Leigh Steinberg);
crusaders/evidence-builders (Ann McKee / BU CTE Center, Chris Nowinski); the
compromised-ally pattern (Julian Bailes — later NFL-affiliated, genuinely
contested whether capture or reform); the human cost (Mike Webster, Dave Duerson,
Junior Seau — Duerson & Seau shot themselves in the chest specifically so their
brains could be studied). **Keep CTE (caused by head trauma, only confirmable
post-mortem) and bipolar disorder (genetic, not caused by football) medically
distinct.**
