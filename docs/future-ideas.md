# Future Book Ideas -- Jack Moon's vault

Ideas the author has kicked around for years. NOT active (The Champ first, then Forgotten
in Jan). Candidates for the open Sept-Dec 2026 novella slots or beyond. Logged 2026-08-13.

## The connective tissue (worth remembering)
Every idea below -- and The Champ + Forgotten -- is the SAME wound in a different costume:
**flawed people, the masks they wear, and the moral cost of power / fame / success.** This is
Jack Moon's authorial identity, the thing that makes a genre-agnostic catalog still read as ONE
author ("flawed men, honest reckonings, any genre"). It's also the read-through/brand solution:
same soul, different genre.

## 1. The Black Market Therapist -- strongest high-concept
A therapist whose clients are bad people: hitmen, mob bosses, cartel bosses, etc.
- Why it's his lane: a therapist SEES BEHIND THE MASK -- the human under the killer. Perfect
  vehicle for the no-villains / everyone's-performing obsession. Naturally episodic (client =
  mask of the week), which fits his interlude-chorus instinct.
- The engine: the therapist's OWN corruption/complicity -- proximity to that power must cost
  them. Are they a fraud too? The therapist's arc has to carry it.
- Trap: client-of-the-week gimmick with no spine. Needs the therapist's own throughline.

## 2. No More Heroes -- great hook, crowded lane
A superhero world where the SUPERVILLAIN WINS, killing off most of the hero population.
- Honest flags: deconstructed/dark-superhero + "villain wins" is well-trodden (The Boys,
  Watchmen, Injustice, Brightburn); prose superhero fiction is a harder sell than screen. Also
  "No More Heroes" is a taken title (video game) -- rename.
- The fresh, his-only angle: skip the war, write the AFTERMATH -- the villain who won and now
  has nothing left to conquer = "winning lets you be your worst self" at world scale. The empty
  victor.

**Developed (2026-08-13):**
- **MC:** one of the last heroes alive; former **protege of the world's strongest hero**. After
  most heroes die, he sets out to find who's responsible for the genocide -- and along the way
  becomes the **keeper/protector of a group of surviving super children.**
- **Powers:** shadow-based. Wears a trench coat to access shadows at will; shadows are
  interconnected pocket dimensions, BUT he can only access shadows **in his line of sight**
  (great built-in limitation -- lean into what he CAN'T reach).
- Craft notes / levers:
  - **The mentor is the biggest lever.** Where is the world's strongest hero -- dead, missing,
    or behind it all? A reveal that the greatest hero was hollow / the genocide was an inside job
    = devastating + pure Jack Moon (fraud, mask, the hero who was never real). Mentor/protege is
    his recurring bond (Chuck+Johnny, Jack+Pat).
  - **Engine: revenge vs. protection PULL AGAINST each other** -- hunting the killer endangers
    the kids. Make them genuinely conflict. The kids are the heart (saves it from cold revenge).
  - **Thematic resonance (free):** a SHADOW-powered protege in the SHADOW of the strongest hero,
    working in the dark, chasing a truth hidden in shadow -- is he a real hero or a shadow of his
    mentor? Use it.
  - **Echo-check (differentiate deliberately):** Logan (last-of-kind protecting a super kid),
    X-Men decimation, noir/Nightcrawler shadow-vigilante. Differentiators = mentor mystery +
    moral/mask themes + revenge-vs-protection.
  - **Unlock question:** who killed the heroes and WHY? Best/most-his answer: the villain is a
    former hero -- one who broke, or was always the monster (ties to villain-who-won thesis).

**Developed further (2026-08-13):**
- **Inciting event:** the world's strongest hero is **assassinated on LIVE TV**; the genocide
  follows fast and violent -- within a week most heroes are dead or missing.
- **The registration law (key worldbuilding + best idea so far):** all super-powered humans must
  register with the government; unregistered = legally a "villain." MC **refuses to conform** --
  lives as a **bounty hunter on the edge of society**, still helps his mentor, sneaks into hero
  HQ to abuse perks; the mentor always covers for him.
  - Triple duty: (1) MC is legally a villain but morally the hero = mask/fraud theme at society
    scale (who assigns the labels); (2) his refusal to register is WHY he survives (he was never
    on the list); (3) **TWIST TO BUILD ON: the registry BECAME the kill list** -- explains how the
    genocide was so fast/total in a week, and why MC is the exception. Build the book on this.
  - Mentor covering for him makes the mentor's death hit harder (his one defender gone; now he's
    exposed as a "villain" with no one to vouch for him).
- **Final villain -- CLAP-based power.** He "claps for his shows"; the more he claps, the more
  power stored in his hands (charge -> unleash). A performer who turns atrocity into THEATER,
  applauds suffering, needs an audience = pure Jack-Moon performance/mask DNA.
  - Connects to the inciting event: the live-TV assassination was his opening SHOW (he applauded).
  - Craft: play him STRAIGHT/rapturous, not campy, or the clap gimmick reads goofy. Applause can
    be his dread-MOTIF sound (like Thump/Flash) -- clapping as horror.
- **Planned finale (movie-brain):** MC fights his way UP A TOWER to the final villain, scored (in
  the author's head) to "Don't Fear the Reaper" (Blue Oyster Cult) as a mood target.
  - Craft flag: a needle-drop is a FILM tool -- in prose you don't get the song, so the writing
    must generate that dread/drive itself. Keep the track as a private mood-compass. And make the
    tower-climb a climb through HIM, not just spectacle (his strength is interior/moral, not set-
    pieces).
- **Echo-check:** registration/"unregistered = villain" is Marvel Civil War / X-Men territory --
  differentiate via registry-as-kill-list + the performance/theater theme + the mentor mystery.

## 3. TBD -- archaeological / supernatural (Indiana-Jones-ish, cerebral)
Less action, more the FINDS and what they MEAN, plus supernatural. First example: the hero
actually finds El Dorado -- and can keep its secrets OR publish for fame and wealth.
- The gold: the secret-vs-fame-and-wealth dilemma = pure Jack Moon (trading truth for glory --
  same wound as Chuck/Jack). That moral choice is the spine.
- Lane: cerebral, atmospheric wonder-and-weight over set-pieces (plays to reflection, his
  strength). Real depth available: archaeology ethics / who owns a discovery / colonialism of
  "finding" sacred treasure -- gives the supernatural a real-world spine.
- Needs: a controlled throughline + defined supernatural rules; the "what it means" payoff must
  land since action isn't carrying it.

## Rough read (author's call)
- Most marketable / freshest: **The Black Market Therapist**.
- Best moral engine: **the El Dorado one**.
- Hardest / most derivative: **No More Heroes** (needs the aftermath angle to stand out).

**Ending inspiration (2026-08-13): Cowboy Bebop.**
- The finale is meant to feel like Bebop's ending -- an ascent to confront the nemesis/past,
  bittersweet, elegiac, COSTLY (Spike's climb to Vicious, the "Bang," the ambiguous death). Pairs
  with "Don't Fear the Reaper" -- both are about facing death at peace.
- Author signature crystallizing: he REFUSES the clean win every time -- the draw (The Champ), the
  happy-ending-that-isn't (Forgotten), Bebop-melancholy here. Across all genres. That's his voice.
- **Key craft insight (his version beats a copy):** Bebop works because Spike had NOTHING left
  (that's why he walks to death at peace). MC has the KIDS -- something to live FOR. So either he
  survives FOR them (more hopeful than Bebop) or DIES to save them (sacrifice, not nihilism).
  Don't copy Spike's emptiness -- MC has something to lose, which is stronger/more painful.
- Notes: earn the melancholy with MC's own past (a Julia-equivalent / the dead heroes / the
  mentor), don't just borrow the vibe. Decide how ambiguous the death is (prose ambiguity is
  powerful but risky -- same note as The Champ's draw ending).

## Cross-idea note: Jack Moon's ending signature
He consistently chooses bittersweet/costly/non-triumphant endings: The Champ (draw, not a win),
Forgotten (redemptive-for-him, tragic-for-the-relationship), No More Heroes (Bebop-melancholy).
This is a defining authorial trait -- part of the "flawed people, honest reckonings" brand.
